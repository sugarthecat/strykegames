Claims

A game/graph theory bot competition.

● Encroach bot 1 Turn 1 Random bot 1 ●

Encroach claims a vertex as close as possible to its opponent. Random claims a random vertex.

What is this?

In the 1980s, Robert Axelrod simulated a tournament between bots submitted by his friends and colleagues for the N-step prisoner's dilemna. I think that's a really cool and fun thing to do, so think of this as an homage (though, a bit different). I saw a tiktok of an AI generated simulation of this game, but the strategies sucked, which led me to wonder: what is a good strategy? I thought it would be a good time to invite all my friends, and some I haven't talked to in a while, to make bots and run a similar tournament to Axelrod.
May the best bots win!

Game Rules

  1. The game begins with a connected graph, and each participant having at least one vertex marked as theirs in the connected graph. As part of the initial conditions, one player is chosen to go first.
  2. For each turn, the player sees the graph, and may select an unclaimed vertex adjacent to one of their claimed vertices, and claim it. If an invalid move is made, or no moves are possible, the player skips their turn.
  3. The game ends when both participants skip their turn.

Scoring

  1. There is a set of initial conditions that will be used to evaluate your bot.
  2. Every bot submitted will compete against every other bot submitted, plus a few reference bots.
  3. For each pair of bots, and graph, the bot's score for the round is its number of claimed vertices.
  4. The initial conditions may be asymmetric. To make this fair, any asymmetric board will be evaluated for both orientations.
  5. Bots, in the end, will be ranked based on their total score across rounds.
  6. The collection of graphs for scoring will be curated, both for graphs representing interesting real-world phenomena, but also for graphs which test important claimer properties.

Submission Guidelines

  1. There will be no more than 1,000 vertices in each graph.
  2. If a bot's total computing time for a single game exceeds 10 seconds (measured on my machine), or crashes, any future moves by the bot in the game will be made by the Random bot.
  3. Only one submission is allowed per person.
  4. Standard Python libraries are allowed.
  5. Bots will be re-initialized for each game.
  6. Bots will be run in python3.
  7. One submission per invite.
  8. No funny business (file accessing, weird system commands).
  9. Submissions submitted before Nov 5th, 2026 will be accepted (If you need more time or something, just ask.).

Submission Format

A single Python file containing one subclass of Claimer (from claimer.py in the starter pack).

from claimer import Claimer

class MyClaimer(Claimer):
    def getNextMove(self):
        # Return an unclaimed vertex adjacent to one of yours,
        # or None to skip your turn.
        ...
  • __init__(graph, myVertices, oppVertices) is called once per game. graph is a dict mapping each vertex to a list of its neighbors. myVertices and oppVertices are sets of each side's starting vertices.
  • getNextMove() is called on each of your turns. An invalid move counts as a skip.
  • claimed(vertex) is called after each of your valid moves, and enemyClaimed(vertex) after each of your opponent's. By default these add the vertex to myVertices and oppVertices; if you override them, call super() or keep track yourself.
  • Vertices can be any hashable value. In the example graphs they are strings.

Testing

The starter pack includes the Random and Encroach bots in strategies/, example graphs in graphs/, and an evaluator. Put your file in strategies/ and play a game with: 

python evaluator.py mybot encroach states
